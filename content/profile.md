I got into computer science through game theory. I wanted to know how optimal play works in games I already loved, like spades and chess. That turned into what I still enjoy most: understanding a system end to end, then taking it apart to fix it, change it or make it do something new.

That ranges from a Linux kernel driver for USB relay boards, which flips hardware switches from a byte written to `/dev`, up to Conturo. Conturo is a product I founded to help nonprofits do better outreach, after seeing the need firsthand as a data engineer at the Center for Puppetry Arts. Before that, I was a software engineering apprentice at Google, where I wrote a chess engine to benchmark a reinforcement-learning agent.

Away from the keyboard I play ultimate frisbee. In 2026 I won gold with the U.S. team at the U-20 world championships.
